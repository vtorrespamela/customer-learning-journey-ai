# Customer Health Score Framework

## Purpose

This framework shows how Customer Success teams can combine customer signals to assess account health, identify risks and opportunities, and prioritize proactive action.

The goal is not to reduce customer health to a single number. The score should help the Customer Success Manager understand what may be happening and decide where human attention is needed.

## Health Dimensions

| Dimension | Healthy Signal | Risk Signal | Critical Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Usage | Consistent usage of key features | Declining or inconsistent usage | Significant drop or inactivity |
| Engagement | Regular interaction with the CS team | Reduced engagement | No meaningful engagement |
| Adoption | Key workflows are being used | Some workflows remain unused | Core workflows are not adopted |
| Customer Outcomes | Customer is progressing toward desired outcomes | Progress is slower than expected | Customer is not achieving expected value |
| Support | Issues are resolved with limited friction | Repeated or unresolved issues | Critical unresolved issues |
| Relationship | Strong stakeholder engagement | Reduced stakeholder participation | Loss of key stakeholder or sponsor |

## Health Assessment

Customer signals should be evaluated together rather than in isolation.

A single negative signal does not necessarily indicate account risk.

For example:

- A temporary decline in usage may be expected during a business cycle.
- Low usage combined with reduced engagement may indicate a stronger adoption risk.
- Strong usage without progress toward business outcomes may indicate a value realization problem.

## Risk Levels

### Healthy

The customer is consistently using the product, engaging with the team, and progressing toward desired outcomes.

Recommended action:

Maintain engagement and identify opportunities to increase value.

### At Risk

The customer shows one or more signals that may indicate friction, declining adoption, or reduced engagement.

Recommended action:

Investigate the underlying cause and create a targeted intervention.

### Critical

Multiple signals indicate significant risk to adoption, customer outcomes, or the relationship.

Recommended action:

Prioritize human intervention, identify the root cause, and establish a clear recovery plan.

## Example

A fitness studio has historically shown consistent SaaS platform usage.

Recent customer signals show:

- Reduced usage of the member engagement features.
- Fewer interactions with the Customer Success team.
- Staff members reporting difficulty using a specific workflow.

Possible interpretation:

The account may be experiencing an adoption barrier rather than general dissatisfaction.

Recommended CS response:

1. Investigate the workflow causing friction.
2. Identify which staff members are affected.
3. Provide targeted enablement.
4. Monitor usage after the intervention.
5. Reassess account health based on the new signals.

## Key Principle

Customer health is a decision-support tool, not a replacement for Customer Success judgment.

The most useful health assessment combines quantitative signals with qualitative context to determine what the customer needs next.
