# Generic vs. Personalized Onboarding

## Purpose

This example demonstrates the difference between a generic onboarding experience and a personalized onboarding approach based on customer context.

The goal is to show how customer information can be used to make onboarding more relevant, actionable, and aligned with the customer's goals.

## Customer scenario

The customer is an operations manager at a growing fitness studio.

They are responsible for coordinating daily operations, supporting staff, monitoring member engagement, and ensuring that the team uses the SaaS platform consistently.

### Customer context

- Goal: Increase team adoption of the platform
- Main challenge: Staff members are still using old processes
- Product familiarity: Basic
- Learning preference: Short practical guidance and examples
- Engagement signal: Low product adoption after onboarding

## Generic onboarding

A generic onboarding experience might include:

- A standard product tour
- A general training session
- A list of available features
- Generic help documentation
- A follow-up email with additional resources

### Potential limitation

Although the customer receives the same information as other customers, the experience does not directly address the team's specific adoption barriers.

The customer may understand what the product does but still not know how to incorporate it into their daily workflow.

## Personalized onboarding

A personalized onboarding experience would use the customer's context to prioritize the most relevant actions.

For this customer, the onboarding could focus on:

1. Identifying the team's current workflow
2. Selecting the product features most relevant to daily operations
3. Creating a simple adoption plan for the staff
4. Providing short, role-specific learning resources
5. Monitoring early engagement signals
6. Adjusting the onboarding experience based on observed behavior

## Example personalized learning plan

### Week 1

Focus on understanding the team's current workflow and introducing the core features required for daily operations.

### Week 2

Introduce practical use cases and encourage staff members to use the platform during their normal routines.

### Week 3

Review adoption signals, identify barriers, and provide targeted support.

### Week 4

Evaluate progress and determine whether additional enablement or intervention is needed.

## Why personalization matters

Personalized onboarding can help connect product capabilities to the customer's actual goals and workflows.

Instead of simply showing customers what the product can do, the experience focuses on helping them understand **why specific features matter to them and how to use them effectively**.

## Customer Success perspective

This approach can help a Customer Success Manager:

- Identify adoption barriers earlier
- Prioritize enablement efforts
- Create more relevant customer experiences
- Support product adoption
- Use customer signals to guide interventions

## Key takeaway

The difference between generic and personalized onboarding is not simply the amount of information provided.

It is the ability to connect the learning experience to the customer's context, goals, challenges, and behavior.

This is where AI-assisted personalization can support Customer Success at scale.
