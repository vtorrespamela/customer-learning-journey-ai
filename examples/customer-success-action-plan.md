# Customer Success Action Plan

## Purpose

This action plan translates customer signals and health indicators into practical Customer Success interventions.

The goal is to define what should happen next, why the action matters, and how success will be measured.

## Customer Situation

A SaaS customer has completed onboarding but is showing inconsistent product adoption.

The customer understands the basic functionality of the platform but has not yet incorporated the product into their team's daily workflows.

Customer signals include:

- Reduced usage of key features.
- Inconsistent engagement from team members.
- Questions about which features should be used regularly.
- Limited progress toward the customer's expected outcomes.

## Main Customer Risk

The primary risk is incomplete product adoption.

The customer may understand how the product works without understanding how to integrate it effectively into their existing processes.

This creates a gap between product usage and customer value.

## Customer Success Objective

Increase meaningful product adoption by helping the customer connect the platform to their daily workflows and desired outcomes.

## Action Plan

### Action 1: Identify Adoption Barriers

What should happen:

Review usage patterns and customer feedback to identify which features or workflows are creating friction.

Why it matters:

Understanding the root cause prevents Customer Success from applying generic training when the actual problem may be workflow design, lack of clarity, or limited stakeholder engagement.

Customer signal:

Inconsistent usage and questions about which features should be used regularly.

Success indicator:

A clear set of adoption barriers is identified and prioritized.

### Action 2: Provide Targeted Enablement

What should happen:

Provide focused guidance based on the customer's specific workflows and adoption barriers.

Why it matters:

Targeted enablement is more likely to change customer behavior than repeating general product training.

Customer signal:

The team completed initial training but is still using previous processes.

Success indicator:

The team begins using the relevant product features in its regular workflow.

### Action 3: Establish a First-Value Milestone

What should happen:

Define a measurable customer outcome that demonstrates meaningful product value.

Why it matters:

A clear milestone gives the customer and the Customer Success Manager a shared definition of progress.

Customer signal:

The customer is using the platform without consistently connecting usage to desired outcomes.

Success indicator:

The customer achieves the agreed first-value milestone.

### Action 4: Monitor Adoption After Intervention

What should happen:

Review product usage and customer engagement after the intervention.

Why it matters:

The effectiveness of a Customer Success action should be evaluated using new customer signals rather than assumptions.

Customer signal:

Previous usage patterns indicate inconsistent adoption.

Success indicator:

Usage of the targeted features becomes more consistent and engagement improves.

## Measurement

The action plan should be evaluated using a combination of quantitative and qualitative signals.

Possible indicators include:

- Increased usage of relevant features.
- Increased consistency of product usage.
- Improved stakeholder engagement.
- Completion of the first-value milestone.
- Positive customer feedback.
- Progress toward the customer's desired outcome.

## Decision Point

After the intervention, reassess the customer situation.

If adoption improves:

Continue supporting the customer toward broader value realization.

If adoption remains low:

Investigate whether the underlying barrier is related to workflow, product fit, stakeholder alignment, or customer capability.

If adoption declines further:

Increase the level of Customer Success intervention and create a structured recovery plan.

## Key Principle

Customer Success actions should be driven by customer signals, connected to customer outcomes, and evaluated through measurable changes in customer behavior or value.

The objective is not simply to increase product usage.

The objective is to help the customer achieve meaningful value from the product.
